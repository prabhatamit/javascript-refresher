THREAT INTELLIGENCE / 01—10

Know the attacker's playbook.

Practical signal → likely risk → defensive move. Choose a topic or search the library.

10 threat briefings
THREAT / 01HIGH IMPACT
↗

Phishing & fake sign-ins

A message impersonates a trusted brand, school, bank or colleague to make you reveal information or open a dangerous link.

DEFENSE MOVE

Open the official app or type the known address yourself. Never share passwords, PINs or one-time codes through a message.

Red flags to notice
  • Unexpected urgency or threats of account closure.
  • Sender address or domain subtly differs from the real one.
  • A link asks you to re-enter credentials without a clear reason.
THREAT / 02ACCOUNT RISK
⌁

Password reuse & credential attacks

Attackers try exposed passwords from one site on other accounts, or guess common patterns at scale.

DEFENSE MOVE

Use a unique password for every account, store it in a reputable password manager, and enable multifactor authentication (MFA).

Red flags to notice
  • Login alerts you do not recognize.
  • Password-reset messages you did not request.
  • Several accounts reuse the same password.
THREAT / 03DATA RISK
✳

Malware & ransomware

A malicious file or app can spy, damage data, or lock files and demand payment.

DEFENSE MOVE

Keep software updated, download from trusted sources, use reputable endpoint protection, and maintain a separate backup you can restore.

Red flags to notice
  • Unexpected attachments or installers.
  • Requests to disable protection or enable macros.
  • Unexpected file changes, pop-ups or security warnings.
THREAT / 04TRUST RISK
◎

Social engineering & impersonation

Someone creates pressure or pretends to be an authority figure to bypass your normal judgment.

DEFENSE MOVE

Verify money transfers, gift-card requests, password resets and sensitive requests using a known number or internal directory—not the details in the message.

Red flags to notice
  • “Keep this secret” or “do it immediately.”
  • A request to bypass normal approval.
  • Unusual tone, payment method or change of bank details.
THREAT / 05LOGIN RISK
⌗

MFA fatigue & verification-code theft

Repeated login prompts or fake support calls try to make you approve access or reveal a code.

DEFENSE MOVE

Reject prompts you did not initiate. Never read out an MFA code. Report repeated requests and use phishing-resistant methods when available.

Red flags to notice
  • Login approvals arrive when you are not signing in.
  • Someone asks you to “confirm” a code over the phone.
  • Repeated prompts continue after you reject them.
THREAT / 06DEVICE RISK
▣

QR codes, USB drives & unsafe downloads

A code, removable drive or download can lead to a fake login page or unwanted software.

DEFENSE MOVE

Check the destination before opening it, get software from official sources, and do not connect unknown USB drives to your device.

Red flags to notice
  • A QR sticker appears to cover another code.
  • A download comes from an unexpected pop-up.
  • A file asks for unusual permissions or protection to be disabled.
THREAT / 07NETWORK RISK
⌁

Unsafe networks & rogue hotspots

A look-alike Wi-Fi name or untrusted network may expose activity or redirect you to fake pages.

DEFENSE MOVE

Confirm the network name with the venue, keep sharing turned off on public networks, and avoid sensitive work on networks you do not trust.

Red flags to notice
  • Several confusingly similar Wi-Fi names.
  • A network demands unusual details before connecting.
  • A login page appears unexpectedly.
THREAT / 08PRIVACY RISK
◉

Digital footprint & data oversharing

Public posts, location details and personal facts can be combined to impersonate you or target your accounts.

DEFENSE MOVE

Review privacy settings, limit app permissions, avoid posting live location or sensitive documents, and remove information you no longer need to share.

Red flags to notice
  • Apps request access unrelated to their function.
  • Public posts reveal routines, addresses or account-recovery clues.
  • Unknown people ask personal questions to “verify” you.
THREAT / 09IMPERSONATION RISK
◌

AI voice, video & message scams

A convincing voice note or video can imitate a friend, family member or executive to trigger a rushed decision.

DEFENSE MOVE

Use a pre-agreed verification phrase or call the person on a known number. Confirm unusual payment or secrecy requests through a second channel.

Red flags to notice
  • Urgent requests that avoid normal verification.
  • Instructions to keep the request secret.
  • Money or credentials requested through an unfamiliar route.
THREAT / 10PERSONAL SAFETY
✳

Cyberbullying & online harassment

Abusive messages, threats or sharing private information can cause real harm beyond the screen.

DEFENSE MOVE

Do not retaliate. Save evidence, block and report the account, tighten privacy settings, and tell a trusted adult or appropriate authority if you feel unsafe.

Red flags to notice
  • Repeated unwanted contact or threats.
  • Someone demands images, money or secrecy.
  • Private information is posted without permission.

ATTACK PATTERN / 4 STAGES

Break the chain early.

Many incidents follow a recognisable sequence. Interrupting even one step can reduce the impact.

01LURE

A message, call, QR code or file gets attention.

02TRUST

The attacker creates urgency or impersonates someone.

03ACTION

A click, approval, download or payment is triggered.

04IMPACT

Account access, data loss, fraud or disruption follows.
